# Unit 5: 1844–1877

* 1845 — Texas Annexation
   * Us officially annexed texas
   * Increased tensions with mexico and intensified disagreements over the expansion of slavery because texas allowed slavery
* 1846–1848 — Mexican-American War
   * War between the us and mexico over texas and territorial expansion
   * Us won and acquired territory
* 1850 — Compromise of 1850
   * The compromise to settle slavery in territories aquired by mexico
   * Cali free, utah and new mexico would choose (pop. sovereignty), slave trade was abolished, and fugitive slave act was strengthened.
* 1854 — Kansas-Nebraska Act
   * Created the kansas and nebraska territories and allowed settlers to decide whether slavery would be permitted through pop. soverignty
   * Repealed the missouri compromise
   * Increased sectinonal tensions and led to violence in kansas
* 1855–1856 — Bleeding Kansas
   * Violence between pro-slavery and anti-slavery settlers who rushed into kansas to win the popular sovereignty vote
   * Pro-slavery "border ruffians" from missouri voted illegally, so kansas ended up with two rival governments
   * Sack of lawrence and john brown's pottawatomie massacre
   * Showed that popular sovereignty couldn't settle slavery peacefully
* 1856 — Caning of Charles Sumner
   * Senator preston brooks of south carolina beat massachusetts senator charles sumner with a cane on the senate floor
   * Came after sumner's "crime against kansas" speech insulting brooks's relative
   * North was outraged, south sent brooks new canes, showed the violence had reached congress itself
* 1857 — Dred Scott v. Sandford
   * Supreme court (chief justice taney) ruled that enslaved people weren't citizens and couldn't sue in federal court
   * Said congress couldn't ban slavery in the territories, so the missouri compromise was unconstitutional
   * Enslaved people were property protected by the 5th amendment
   * Angered the north and helped the new republican party grow
* 1859 — John Brown’s Raid
   * Abolitionist john brown attacked the federal arsenal at harpers ferry, virginia, hoping to arm enslaved people for a revolt
   * Raid failed, brown was captured and hanged for treason
   * Northerners saw him as a martyr, southerners saw proof that the north wanted to destroy slavery by force
* 1860 — Election of 1860
   * Abraham lincoln (republican) won without winning a single southern electoral vote
   * Democrats split between douglas (north) and breckinridge (south), bell ran for the constitutional union party
   * Lincoln promised to stop the spread of slavery to the territories, not end it where it existed
   * South carolina seceded in december 1860, and 6 more deep south states followed before lincoln took office
* 1861 — Fort Sumter / Civil War Begins
   * Confederate forces fired on fort sumter, a federal fort in charleston harbor, in april 1861
   * Lincoln called for 75,000 volunteers, so virginia, north carolina, tennessee, and arkansas seceded too
   * Border states (maryland, kentucky, missouri, delaware) stayed in the union
* 1862 — Homestead Act
   * Gave 160 acres of western land to settlers who lived on it and farmed it for 5 years
   * Passed once southern democrats left congress, along with the pacific railway act and morrill land grant act
   * Encouraged westward settlement but took more land from native americans
* 1863 — Emancipation Proclamation
   * Issued by lincoln after the union win at antietam, took effect january 1 1863
   * Freed enslaved people only in confederate states in rebellion, not the border states
   * Changed the war's goal to ending slavery and kept britain and france from helping the confederacy
   * Let black men join the union army, around 180,000 served
* 1865 — Lee Surrenders / Civil War Ends
   * Robert e lee surrendered to ulysses s grant at appomattox court house in april 1865
   * Lincoln was assassinated by john wilkes booth days later, andrew johnson became president
   * Union preserved, slavery ended, about 620,000 dead
* 1865 — Freedmen’s Bureau
   * Federal agency that helped freed people and poor whites after the war
   * Gave food, medical care, set up schools and colleges like howard and fisk, helped with labor contracts
   * Underfunded and ended in 1872
* 1865 — Black Codes
   * Laws passed by southern states to control freed people
   * Required labor contracts, vagrancy laws let them arrest unemployed black people and force them to work
   * Kept them from voting, serving on juries, or owning certain land, basically slavery in a new form
   * Made radical republicans angry and pushed congress to take over reconstruction
* 1865 — Sharecropping
   * Freed people and poor whites farmed land owned by someone else in exchange for a share of the crop
   * Crop lien system: borrowed seeds and tools against the next harvest, so they fell into a cycle of debt
   * Kept many black families tied to the land and poor for generations
* 1865 — Ku Klux Klan Founded
   * Founded in pulaski, tennessee by confederate veterans
   * Used terror, violence, and lynching against black americans and white republicans to stop them from voting
   * Congress passed the enforcement acts / ku klux klan act (1870–1871) to fight it
* 1865 — 13th Amendment
   * Abolished slavery in the united states
   * Except as punishment for a crime, a loophole later used for convict leasing
* 1867 — Reconstruction Acts
   * Radical republicans took over reconstruction from johnson
   * Split the south into 5 military districts under union generals
   * Southern states had to write new constitutions that let black men vote and ratify the 14th amendment to rejoin
   * Johnson vetoed them and congress overrode him; he was impeached in 1868 and acquitted by one vote
* 1868 — 14th Amendment
   * Gave citizenship to everyone born or naturalized in the us, overturning dred scott
   * States had to give equal protection of the laws and due process
   * Later used in cases like brown v board of education
* 1870 — 15th Amendment
   * Said the right to vote couldn't be denied because of race, color, or previous servitude
   * Left women out, which split the women's rights movement
   * Southern states later got around it with poll taxes, literacy tests, and grandfather clauses
* 1877 — Compromise of 1877
   * Disputed election between rutherford b hayes (republican) and samuel tilden (democrat)
   * Democrats let hayes become president if federal troops left the south
   * Ended reconstruction, "redeemer" democrats took back southern governments and jim crow laws followed

# Thesis Sentence Frames

* Causation
   * [Event/development] was primarily caused by [cause 1] and [cause 2], which led to [effect].
   * Although [cause A] played a role, [cause B] was the most significant cause of [event] because [reason].
   * [Event] led to [effect 1] and [effect 2], as shown by [example 1] and [example 2].
* Continuity and Change (CCOT)
   * From [year] to [year], [topic] changed as [change], although [continuity] stayed the same because [reason].
   * While [topic] changed significantly through [change 1] and [change 2], [continuity] remained because [reason].
* Comparison
   * While both [A] and [B] [similarity], they differed in [difference] because [reason].
   * [A] and [B] were similar in that [similarity], but [A] [difference] whereas [B] [difference].
* Extent / Evaluate
   * To a large extent, [claim], as seen in [category 1] and [category 2], although [limit or counterpoint].
   * Although [counterpoint], [claim] was largely true because [reason 1] and [reason 2].
* Complexity add-on (use with any thesis)
   * …; however, [counterargument], which shows that [more nuanced claim].
   * …, but this change was limited because [group/region] still [continuity].

# Contextualization Frames

* Before [period/event], [broader development], which set the stage for [topic].
* In the years leading up to [year], [trend/conflict] created tension over [issue], leading to [topic of the prompt].
* [Earlier development] had already [effect], so by [year] Americans were [situation], which explains why [topic].

# Commentary Sentence Frames

* Explaining evidence
   * This shows that [point] because [explanation].
   * This demonstrates how [evidence] [caused/changed/proved] [result], which supports the argument that [thesis claim].
   * This is significant because it led to [effect], which [connects to thesis].
   * As a result, [consequence], which reveals that [larger point about the period].
   * In other words, [restate evidence in your own words], meaning that [analysis].
* Linking back to the thesis
   * Therefore, [evidence] proves that [thesis claim].
   * This connects to the larger pattern of [theme], showing that [claim].
   * By [action], [group] was able to [result], which reflects [thesis idea].
* Sourcing (HIPP: Historical situation, Intended audience, Purpose, Point of view)
   * Because the author was [position/identity], they likely [view/purpose], which affects the document because [reason].
   * The historical situation of [event/context] explains why [author] argued [claim].
   * [Author] was writing to [intended audience], so they [emphasized/left out] [detail] in order to [purpose].
   * The purpose of this document was to [purpose], which helps the argument because it shows [point].
* Complexity
   * However, [counter-evidence], which complicates the argument because [explanation].
   * While [claim] was true for [group/region], it was not true for [other group/region] because [reason].
   * This change was limited, though, since [continuity] still [remained/persisted] because [reason].
